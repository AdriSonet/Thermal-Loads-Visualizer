# Thermal loads visualizer

An interactive 3D look at the physical processes behind a room's thermal load: sun through the glazing, absorption, conduction and storage in the slab, long-wave exchange, convection, people, lights, equipment, ventilation and the system load.

Every number and glyph follows a simulated peak day (summer and winter, 3-minute time steps) of a 12 × 8 × 4 m boarding lounge in Zaragoza with a 9 × 3 m south-facing curtain wall. The few values that are estimated for the drawing (the sunlit floor temperature, for example) are marked as estimated on the page.

## Open it

Serve the folder with any static server and open it in a browser:

```bash
python -m http.server 5181
```

Then go to <http://localhost:5181/>. Three.js loads from a CDN, so an internet connection is needed.

## Using it

- **Story** is the lecture: nine chapters from A to Z (the problem, outdoor conditions, envelope, internal gains, surfaces, storage, room air, system, results). Each chapter opens with what it covers and closes with what to take away and a check question; the answer appears on click.
- **Elements** is the exercise: pick one element (glazing, floor, a wall, people…) and follow every process it takes part in through the day.
- **Concepts** is the review: one idea at a time, with 2D sections of the slab, a wall and the glazing.
- Under each step, **See in Conditions** opens the matching item in the Conditions tab with its explanation.
- Right column: surface temperature scale, the **Room balance** (per time step or per day) and the **Conditions** (outdoors, indoors, internal gains and their radiant, convective, visible and latent shares).
- Keys: ← → steps, Space play/pause, F full screen, L navigator.

## Files

- `index.html`: the whole visualizer.
- `data.js`: the simulated day, generated by `export.py`.
- `shoebox.py`, `export.py`, `shoebox.hbjson`: how the model and the data were made. They rely on the ThermalLoads toolchain, which is not part of this repository.

This is a look test: a prototype to iterate on the visual language, not a finished tool.
